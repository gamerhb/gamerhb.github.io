Visionaire Next-Gen Smart Glasses.

An early-stage product/business-plan project centered on pricing, financing, startup economics, revenue scenarios, and break-even reasoning.

Evidence boundary: the original business-plan and pitch files have not been recovered. This page uses figures preserved in prior resume evidence and does not present reconstructed material as an original artifact.

What I owned

Within the four-person team, my documented ownership centered on pricing, financing, startup-cost assumptions, revenue and expense projections, and break-even analysis.

The business-model question

The useful part of the project was not the headset concept by itself. It was translating a speculative product into a set of economic questions: what price could support the cost structure, how many units would be required to recover fixed investment, and how sensitive the business became to adoption.

Recorded planning range

25,000–45,000 units over three years · $10.75M–$19.35M projected revenue · $7.525M–$13.545M projected gross profit.

The preserved project evidence also records a 34,150-unit break-even point and a modeled 60–80% ROI range. These were project assumptions/projections, not realized commercial outcomes.

What belongs in the portfolio

Because the source files are missing, I would rather show the ownership boundary and the preserved economics than manufacture a polished investor deck after the fact. If the original files are recovered, this page can be upgraded with the real business-plan and pitch artifacts.

What the project demonstrates

Even as earlier work, it shows a pattern that continued in later projects: turning a broad idea into explicit variables, thresholds, and decision constraints so the discussion can move from “is this exciting?” to “what would have to be true for this to work?”
