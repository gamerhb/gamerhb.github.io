Experience / N&M Events
On-call family business · Jun 2023–Present

Live Event Operations: Make the Plan Work in Real Time.

Setup, layouts, equipment, transportation, inventory, timelines, and client/team coordination across more than 50 private, cultural, and corporate events ranging from roughly 10 to 200 attendees.

Work context: this is on-call/as-needed work in a family business, not a full-time continuous role.

The work is physical, logistical, and time-sensitive

Events compress a lot of dependencies into a short window. Equipment has to arrive, layouts have to fit the space, décor and staging need to be assembled, inventory has to be accounted for, and changes from the client or venue have to be absorbed without losing the timeline.

My role

I support décor/setup, floor layouts, equipment movement, transportation, inventory, timeline execution, and coordination with the team and client as needs change. The exact mix depends on the event.

Before the eventLoad, transport, inventory, equipment, and setup preparation.
On siteLayout execution, staging, décor placement, and coordination around space constraints.
During changesAdjust the setup or sequence when client, venue, or timing requirements shift.
AfterwardBreakdown, equipment recovery, inventory, and transport.

Why this experience matters to me

Most of my portfolio is analytical or systems-oriented. Event work is a useful counterweight because the feedback loop is immediate: either the room is ready, the equipment is where it needs to be, and the team can execute—or it is not.

There is less room to hide behind a polished plan once the event starts. That makes prioritization, communication, and practical coordination visible very quickly.

What I carried into other work

I am more comfortable with plans that include a fallback, clear ownership, and enough operational slack to absorb changes. The same instinct appears later in my software and finance work: design for the path that actually occurs, not only the clean version drawn at the beginning.
